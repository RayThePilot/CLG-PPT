# HomeBuddy: Presentation Script

Team: **Jatin** (lead, live demo), **Shivansh** (business), **Ajay** (risks and close)
Deck: `presentation/HomeBuddy-final.pptx` (18 slides). Prototype: `homebuddy/` running on your laptop.

**How the time is split:** Jatin speaks the most (story, product, live demo). Shivansh speaks a medium amount (money and competition). Ajay speaks the least (two short parts he can say from memory).

| Speaker | Slides | Words | Share | Speaking time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jatin | 1 to 12 and the first line of 18 | 1,076 | 76% | about 8 min (the live demo adds clicks and pauses) |
| Shivansh | 13 to 16 | 268 | 19% | about 2 min |
| Ajay | 17, and two lines on 18 | 79 | 6% | under 1 min |

Total: about 11 to 12 minutes with the live demo. If you need it shorter, skip slide 5, shorten slide 8, and drop the phone moment in slide 11.

**How to read this script**
- Lines in `>` blocks are the words to say. Everything in *italics* is an action, not something you say.
- Slide animations start by themselves when you reach a slide. Say your first line, then let the slide finish appearing. Advance with the right arrow key.
- Do not read the slides. The slide shows the point, you tell the story.
- Slow down on numbers. Pause for two seconds after every question.
- The Hindi lines are written in English letters (Hinglish) and are in Jatin's parts only. Say them with a smile, like a joke you share with the room. If a guest or judge does not speak Hindi, say the English line and skip the Hindi one. The meanings are in the table at the end.

## Before you start (5-minute checklist)

1. Start the prototype: open a terminal in `homebuddy/` and run `npm run dev`. Open the link it prints.
2. Fresh start: in Chrome press F12, go to Application, Storage, and click "Clear site data". Reload. This resets chats and saved items to the demo data.
3. Open three browser tabs in this order: landing page `/`, then `/app/places`, then keep the quiz for later. Zoom 100%.
4. Put the phone (or Chrome device mode) ready with the same link, for the 15-second mobile moment.
5. Turn off notifications. Plug in the charger. Close other apps.
6. Open the PPT in slide show mode. Keep the prototype in a second window so you can switch with Alt+Tab.
7. **Backup plan:** slides 9 to 12 already contain real screenshots. If the demo breaks, say: "Our demo is on the next slide as screenshots, let me walk you through it", and keep going.

## The script

### Slide 1: Title (Jatin, about 25 seconds)

*Stand still. Wait until the room is quiet. Smile.*

**JATIN**
> Quick show of hands. Who here has ever moved to a new city with one bag and zero friends?
> *(pause, look around)*
> Keep your hand up if you paid a broker a full month of rent just to get a key.
> *(pause)*
> Okay. Hands down. Agar aapka haath upar tha, toh yeh project aapke liye hai. That is exactly why we built HomeBuddy.
> I am Jatin, with Shivansh and Ajay. In the next few minutes we will show you a startup that finds your place, and your people.

---

### Slide 2: The problem (Jatin, about 40 seconds)

**JATIN**
> Picture this. You land in a new city. It is nine at night, your phone is at twelve percent, and a broker says, "Pehle ek mahine ka kiraya do, baaki baad mein dekhenge." Pay one month of rent first.
> So you pay. You get a room. And then you meet your flatmate. He wakes up at 6 AM and you sleep at 3. Ek subah jaldi uthta hai, ek raat bhar jaagta hai. He cooks things you cannot stand. He has friends over every night.
> Three things go wrong. Your money disappears into brokerage. Strangers turn out to be bad matches. And the whole search is scattered across ten WhatsApp groups where you cannot tell who is real.
> Here is the gap. Apps today list rooms, or they list people. Not one of them does both.

---

### Slide 3: How many people (Jatin, about 45 seconds)

**JATIN**
> How big is this? In India, 4.33 crore students are enrolled in higher education. And the census counted 4.14 crore people who moved between states for work. Those numbers come from the government's higher education survey and the census.
> Now we want to be honest with you. No official number says how many of them struggle to find a good home. So we plan a short class survey to measure that.
> But look at what one person loses. On a ten thousand rupee room, one month of brokerage is ten thousand rupees. That is about eight percent of a whole year of rent, gone before you sleep there even once. Sirf chaabi ke liye, ek saal ke kiraye ka aath percent.

---

### Slide 4: Why it matters long term (Jatin, about 50 seconds)

**JATIN**
> And it does not end after a month. A study of college roommates found that low mood and low grades actually spread between close roommates. As one person's mood fell, the roommate's grades fell too.
> Another review found that poor sleep hurts focus, memory and marks. And students who start with depressive symptoms tend to get lower grades and are more likely to drop out in the first two years.
> Hamare yahan kehte hain, sangat ka asar padta hai. Research bhi yahi kehti hai.
> Fair warning, most of this research is from abroad. It shows the pattern, not an Indian number. But the lesson is simple.
> Who you live with is not a small decision. Matching people well is prevention, not a luxury.

---

### Slide 5: Who it is for (Jatin, about 20 seconds)

**JATIN**
> So who is this for? Everyone who moves away from home. Students, interns, first-job workers, people changing cities, even exam aspirants in Kota.
> And on the other side, PG owners and landlords with empty beds who just want tenants they can trust. Two sides, one platform. Chahe Kota ka student ho ya Bengaluru ka intern, ghar sabko chahiye.

---

### Slide 6: Our solution (Jatin, about 35 seconds)

**JATIN**
> Meet HomeBuddy. You take a two-minute lifestyle quiz. We rank verified homes and flatmates by a score you can actually understand.
> Four ideas hold it together. The quiz. Verified profiles. Matching the place and the people together. And Squad Up, where people who match team up and rent a bigger flat for less each.
> Ghar se door, ghar jaisa. HomeBuddy: aapka ghar, aapke log.

---

### Slide 7: How it works (Jatin, about 15 seconds)

**JATIN**
> Three steps. Tell us how you live. Meet your matches. Move in with confidence. Bas teen kadam. In one minute you will see all three, live.

---

### Slide 8: The matching engine (Jatin, about 40 seconds)

**JATIN**
> This is not a random number. It is six factors, and each one is weighted by how often it starts fights.
> Cleanliness counts most, twenty-two percent. Then sleep rhythm at twenty. Guests and noise, sixteen. Budget, sixteen. Smoking and drinking, fourteen. Food habits, twelve.
> The weights are our starting assumption, and we would tune them with real user feedback. And every score comes with reasons in plain words. No black box. Koi jaadu nahi, sab saaf saaf.
> Enough slides. Let me show you.

---

### Slides 9 to 12: Live demo (Jatin, about 3 minutes)

*Switch to the browser window with Alt+Tab. Keep the slide deck on slide 9 behind it, so you can jump back if needed.*

**JATIN**

**Slide 10: Dashboard and profiles (about 55 seconds)**

**JATIN**

**Slide 11: Places, filters, messages, saved (about 55 seconds)**

**JATIN**

> *(click "Request a visit")*
> And a visit request opens a chat with the owner. She replies right here.
> *(click the bookmark icon at the top)*
> Anything I like, I save, so I can compare later.

> And it works on a phone too, not just a laptop.

*Click "Squad Up" in the menu.*

**Slide 12: Squad Up (about 40 seconds)**

**JATIN**
> My favourite feature. Squad Up. I add Riya, Aarav and Tanvi, and HomeBuddy shows our group harmony, eighty-one percent. This grid shows who fits with whom.
> *(scroll to Rent split, change a room to "Master + bath")*
> Now the rent. Bigger rooms pay a little more, smaller rooms a little less. The split updates instantly, and it is fair. Ek click mein poora hisaab.
> *(switch back to the slides, slide 13)*
> What you saw is a working prototype. The logic is real. The cities, people and homes are sample data. Product toh ban gaya, but a product is not a business. Shivansh, who pays, and who are we up against?

---

### Slide 13: Market (Shivansh, about 30 seconds)

**SHIVANSH**
> Thank you, Jatin. Our market is people who move, every single year. Students, interns, first-job professionals, people relocating, and exam aspirants.
> How do we size it? People who move, times those who rent shared housing, times those who close through us, times our fee. For example, a thousand rentals a month at seven hundred and fifty rupees is seven and a half lakh a month, from success fees alone. These are assumptions, and a survey will check them.

---

### Slide 14: Competition table (Shivansh, about 25 seconds)

**SHIVANSH**
> Look at this grid. Property portals list homes but do not match flatmates. Social groups are free but nobody is verified. Co-living brands are managed but cost more.
> HomeBuddy is the only row with every tick: homes, flatmates, verified people, group renting, and add-on services.

---

### Slide 15: Business model (Shivansh, about 35 seconds)

**SHIVANSH**
> Our model is simple. Free to match, and we earn when homes get filled.
> Owners pay a small fee per vacancy. A small success fee when a rental closes, between five hundred and a thousand rupees, far below a month of brokerage. An optional Plus plan, around a hundred and forty-nine rupees a month. And a small commission on partners like tiffin, furniture and movers.
> All of these prices are starting assumptions that we will test.

---

### Slide 16: Competitors in detail (Shivansh, about 35 seconds)

**SHIVANSH**
> Quickly, row by row. Portals show you the room, we show you who you would live with. Groups give you posts, we give you verified profiles. Co-living brands give set prices, we give choice, and you pick your people. And brokers charge fifteen days to a month of rent, we charge a small fee only when the rental closes.
> Every startup has risks. Ajay will tell you ours, honestly.

---

### Slide 17: Risks (Ajay, about 35 seconds)

*Ajay: take a breath, look at the slide, then look at the audience. Say it slowly. You only need these four points.*

**AJAY**
> Our biggest risk is safety. So we verify IDs for people and owners, and chats stay inside the app.
> Second, a new platform starts with no users and no listings. So we start small and add owners by hand first.
> Third, people might skip us to avoid fees. So we keep giving value after matching, like house rules and rent split.
> And we collect very little data and follow the rules.

---

### Slide 18: Close (Jatin, then Ajay, then all three, about 25 seconds)

**JATIN**
> Remember the hands we raised at the start? Every one of those people deserves a home they can live in, and flatmates they actually like. Har haath ko ghar milna chahiye, aur dost bhi.

**AJAY**
> Thank you. We are ready for your questions.

*All three, together, looking at the audience:*

**ALL THREE**
> Find your place. Find your people. Ghar se door, ghar jaisa.

## Questions and answers

Pick the person who knows it best. Ajay answers only the two safety questions so he can answer from what he already said.

| Question | Who answers | Short answer to say |
|---|---|---|
| How is this different from NoBroker or Housing.com? | Shivansh | They are great at listings. We match the people and the place together, and we explain the score. That is the gap. |
| How do you make money? | Shivansh | Owners pay per vacancy, a small success fee when a rental closes, an optional Plus plan, and partner commissions. Searching and matching stay free. |
| Is the prototype real? | Jatin | The logic is real: six weighted factors, live filters, chat, saved items and the rent split. The cities, people and homes are sample data, and it has no server yet. |
| How accurate is the score? | Jatin | It is a starting model. The weights come from which habits cause the most conflict. We would tune it with real feedback after launch. |
| Where did your numbers come from? | Jatin | Students from the government's higher education survey, migrants from the census, brokerage from news and property sites, and the research from published studies. Prices and fees are our assumptions. |
| How do you keep people safe? | Ajay | We verify IDs for people and owners, chats stay inside the app, and users can report and review each other. |
| What about privacy? | Ajay | We collect only what we need, we ask for consent, and phone numbers stay private until someone chooses to share. |
| Where would you launch first? | Shivansh | One locality near colleges and offices, with owners added by hand. We will pick the city after we confirm demand. |

**If you do not know an answer:** say "Good question. We have not tested that yet, and it is on our list." Never guess a number.

## Delivery tips

- **Jatin:** you carry the energy. Pause after each question. On the demo, narrate what you click before you click it. If something loads slowly, keep talking.
- **Shivansh:** slow down on the rupee numbers. Look at the audience on the "only row with every tick" line.
- **Ajay:** you only have two short parts. Practise them out loud five times and you will not need to read. If you feel nervous, look at the slide, then at one friendly face.
- **Everyone:** do not rush. A pause feels longer to you than to the audience.
